**Paper → Product in 24h** picks one fresh arXiv paper every day, posted in the last few days, and writes the startup it could become, as if a founder had 24 hours to decide whether to build it. Each memo explains the paper in plain words, says what it actually makes possible, specs a product with a 2-week MVP, proposes who would pay and how, checks the moat, lists the risks and kill criteria, and ends with a verdict.

## Ground rules

- **The paper is the source of truth.** Every claim about a paper links to its arXiv abstract page or its released code. We report results the way the authors report them, including the caveats.
- **No invented market numbers.** Any market size or price figure links to a real source. Otherwise it's labelled a guess. Pricing is always labelled a **hypothesis**.
- **One paper, one memo.** We never repeat an arXiv ID, and we don't pick from the same field two days in a row.
- **Licenses matter.** We check the code, model and dataset licenses, because "open" research often can't be sold as-is.
- **Not advice.** These are product thought experiments, not investment advice, and not endorsements by the paper's authors.

## How we score

| Verdict | Score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Business** | 7–10 | Could support a standalone company: a clear buyer with budget, a repeatable job, and room for a moat. |
| **Feature** | 4–6.5 | Valuable, but it belongs inside someone else's product or platform; hard to sell alone. |
| **Demo** | 0–3.5 | Impressive, but not reliable, cheap or general enough to sell yet. |

The score adds up five parts, each 0–2: **Pain** (how badly someone needs it), **Readiness** (how close the research is to production), **Buyer** (a clear payer with budget), **Moat** (data, workflow or distribution advantage), **Licensing and build risk** (2 = clean and simple). Half points are allowed.
