## TL;DR

- **The paper:** [Adaptive Reliability Reserves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779) asks how much charge a fleet of home batteries should keep back for outages while it trades energy in the Texas market. In a Houston 2024 replay, a rule that raises the reserve when a county outage forecast crosses a threshold beats a fixed 20% reserve on modeled margin; learned policies mostly don't.
- **The product:** **ReserveLab**: a backtesting service for residential battery fleet operators that replays their reserve rules against real prices, outages and weather alerts and reports trading revenue against the customer backup promise.
- **The verdict: Feature · 5.5/10.** Real pain and a clean MIT release, but the buyers are a short list of operators with in-house optimisation, so it fits inside a VPP or DERMS platform.

## The paper in plain words

A home battery has two jobs that compete for the same stored energy: earning money by discharging during price spikes, and keeping the lights on when the grid fails. Every kilowatt-hour sold is one that isn't there if the outage comes next. The paper, by Maruthi Vemula (UNC Chapel Hill) and Neeraj Praneeth Gajula (NYU) ([arXiv:2611.34779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)), builds a careful replay of this trade-off in Texas and asks a narrow question: how much market value can a reserve keep while protecting a five-hour backup promise?

The **key idea** is a fair test bench, not a new algorithm. HomeFleet-ERCOT combines observed ERCOT prices and county-level outage records with simulated households, and runs every policy on the same replay ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). Policies include fixed reserves (10%, 20%, 30%, 50%), a "storm rule" that charges toward full on weather alerts, a risk-priced dynamic program, a hazard-threshold rule that raises the reserve to each home's estimated five-hour need when a county outage forecast crosses a threshold, and 21 reinforcement-learning (PPO) training runs ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). The authors stress that the outage forecast is a county event forecast, not a household failure probability ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)).

**The evidence.** In the Houston 2024 replay with 100 simulated homes, a fixed 20% reserve serves the first five hours of 75.0% of simulated outage episodes. The hazard-threshold rule (ε = 0.1) reaches 86.5% and adds **$10.2 per home per year** in modeled margin (paired 95% interval: +1.7 to +21.7). However, the 11.4-point coverage gain has an interval of [+0.0, +16.7] that includes zero ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). Across the ten "promise-cost" PPO candidates, backup coverage ranges from 31.1% to 96.3%. All 10 earn less modeled margin than fixed 20%, and 3 also have lower coverage ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). The gains don't transfer cleanly: on Houston 2025 the threshold rule earns +$5.1 per home-year but serves 93.7% against 95.8% for fixed 20%, and in San Antonio 2024 it serves 90.9% against 97.7% ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). An alert-triggered charging override rescues the learned policy during Winter Storm Uri: on the Houston Uri window its coverage rises from 69.7% to 98.0% ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). But across 41 county episodes in six counties (2019–2025) where at least 5% of customers lost power, an alert was active at onset in 85.4% of cases, and continuous warning of four hours or more was available in only **19.5%** ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)).

**Limitations, as the authors state them.** This is "a conditional simulation result, not measured company reliability" ([abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). Household loads come from NREL ResStock simulations, individual outages are synthesised from county fractions, and missing county reports count as zero ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). The economics leave out capital, hedging, subscriptions, ancillary-service revenue and battery aging costs. The authors write that neither favourable replays nor their conditional proposition "validates unattended deployment" ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). The release notes that the hardware and tariff settings reproduce a publicly described setting, with no telemetry from Base Power or any other operator ([NOTICE](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet/blob/main/NOTICE)).

**Licenses.** The authored code is **MIT** ([GitHub](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet)). Release v0.2.0 adds 22 replay bundles, all 21 PPO checkpoints and 473 evaluation CSVs ([release](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet/releases/tag/v0.2.0)). Third-party inputs keep their own terms: EAGLE-I outage records and Open-Meteo weather are **CC BY 4.0** ([data card](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet/blob/main/docs/DATA_CARD.md)). ResStock and SMART-DS come from NREL's Open Energy Data Initiative, whose pages say content is CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise noted ([ResStock on OEDI](https://data.openei.org/submissions/4520)). The checkpoints have no separate license file, so we read them as covered by the repository's MIT terms, which still needs confirming. We found **no non-commercial terms**. Attribution (CC BY) is the main obligation.

## What it actually enables

- **A shared yardstick for reserve policies.** Fixed, storm, risk-adaptive and learned controllers on the same causal replay, with paired intervals rather than single headline numbers ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)).
- **Pricing the backup promise.** Margin and five-hour coverage side by side show what each extra point of coverage costs before it goes into a customer contract.
- **Auditing weather-alert strategies.** The warning-coverage audit (only 19.5% of mass-outage episodes with four or more hours of continuous warning) gives operators a way to test whether "charge on alert" features would actually have filled batteries in time ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)).
- **Cheap, CPU-only experimentation.** The 21 PPO runs took 15.3 hours of summed training-loop time on an ARM64 CPU workstation, and an eight-home demo runs without a data download ([README](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet)).
- **Not yet:** a validated reliability number for a real fleet. Without household outage telemetry, real load during storms and real dispatch logs, the replay compares policies but can't certify any of them, and it ignores feeder constraints in dispatch.

## The product

**Name and one-liner.** **ReserveLab**: "Backtest your battery fleet's backup promise before the next storm does."

**User.** Primary: the dispatch, product or risk lead at a company operating a residential battery fleet in a wholesale market. In Texas that means retail electric providers and aggregators in ERCOT's Aggregate Distributed Energy Resource (ADER) pilot ([ERCOT ADER](https://www.ercot.com/mktrules/pilots/ader/)), and operators such as Base Power, which publicly describes a 20% reserve target ([Base Power](https://www.basepowercompany.com/blog/how-base-charges-and-discharges-its-batteries)). Secondary: battery OEMs tuning storm features.

**Job-to-be-done.** *When* I change how my fleet holds back charge for outages, *I want to* see what that change would have done to trading margin and to customers' backup over past storms and price spikes, *so I can* ship the policy, and the marketing claim about it, with evidence instead of a guess.

**Core features.**

- **Policy replay**: upload a reserve policy (rule parameters, or a containerised controller behind a simple API) and replay it against years of prices, county outages and weather alerts for the operator's service areas.
- **Trade-off report**: margin versus five-hour (or custom) backup coverage, with paired bootstrap intervals against the operator's current policy, in the style of the paper's Table 1 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)).
- **Storm drill**: named-event replays (Uri, Elliott, Beryl) and synthetic tests for short, missed and false alerts.
- **Alert-lead audit**: per county, how often warnings arrived early enough to fill each battery model.
- **Own-data mode**: swap simulated homes for the operator's anonymised telemetry and outage logs, which is where the replay becomes credible.

**MVP in 2 weeks.**

- **Days 1–3:** Install the MIT-licensed HomeFleet package, run the demo and full Houston evaluation, and reproduce the published Table 1 numbers from the release ([README](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet)). Wrap it in a job API.
- **Days 4–6:** Build the policy interface: parameterised rules (fixed floor, storm rule, threshold ε, per-home multiplier) plus a Python plug-in contract for custom controllers.
- **Days 7–9:** Trade-off report: margin and coverage with intervals, named-event cards and alert-lead audit, as PDF.
- **Days 10–12:** Add the DFW and San Antonio bundles and a CSV importer for an operator's own logs.
- **Days 13–14:** Demo to 3–5 fleet operators or ADER participants: run their current reserve rule and two alternatives live, then ask whether they'd share telemetry for an own-data pilot. **Ship at the end:** a hosted beta and a public sample report on the Houston replay, with the paper's caveats stated up front.

## Who pays and how

**Buyer.** The head of trading, dispatch or product at residential battery fleet operators and VPP aggregators, paid from the trading-analytics or product budget. The pool is real but small. ERCOT reported **293 MW** of qualified ADER capacity, **9 ADERs participating plus 4 in progress**, and a **500 MW** pilot limit as of its September 30, 2026 update ([ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/30/ERCOT-ADER-Update-Senate-Business-and-Commerce-September-30-2026.pdf)). Base Power says it serves **20,000+ homes** ([Base Power](https://www.basepowercompany.com/blog/how-base-charges-and-discharges-its-batteries)). We found no reliable figure for what these companies spend on simulation or analytics tools, so we don't give one.

**What they use today.** In-house optimisation and backtesting teams; built-in OEM features such as Tesla's Storm Watch, which charges to maximum capacity during qualifying weather warnings ([Tesla](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/powerwall/mobile-app/storm-watch)); proprietary outage prediction (Base Power says it uses "outage prediction technology" and that its batteries can back up "over 97% of outages" ([Base Power](https://www.basepowercompany.com/blog/how-base-charges-and-discharges-its-batteries))); and DERMS/VPP platforms such as EnergyHub ([EnergyHub](https://www.energyhub.com/)).

**Pricing hypothesis (unvalidated):**

- **Policy review**: a fixed-fee engagement per policy change, covering replay, storm drill and a signed-off report, in the low tens of thousands of dollars (guess).
- **Subscription**: an annual license per operator for continuous replay as new seasons and storms arrive, priced by fleet size, roughly $2–5 per home per year (guess). The paper's own modeled gain for the threshold rule is $10.2 per home-year in one replay ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)), which sets a soft ceiling on what a fleet would pay per home.
- **OEM / platform license**: embed the replay engine in a DERMS or VPP platform, which is the likeliest real revenue path (see verdict).

Every number above is a hypothesis to test with design partners, not a market estimate.

## Moat check

| Moat | Strength | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Data | Weak → Medium | The public replay data is open to everyone. Operator telemetry plus real outage outcomes would be valuable and hard to get, but only if operators share it. |
| Workflow lock-in | Medium | If policies are reviewed and claims signed off here, it gains a compliance-style hold. Until then it is a report. |
| Distribution | Weak | The buyer list is short and well networked. Platforms and OEMs already own the relationship. |
| Technical lead | Weak | The benchmark is MIT-licensed and well documented; a capable quant team can rebuild it in weeks. |
| Licensing/IP | Weak | Permissive licenses help us build but give no exclusivity; nothing here is patented as far as we know. |

**Who could copy this fastest:** DERMS and VPP platform vendors (EnergyHub and peers) and the larger fleet operators' own trading teams, who already hold the telemetry and the dispatch code.

## Build stack

- **Model and inference:** the HomeFleet dynamic-programming controllers, threshold rules and PPO policies, plus the paper's gradient-boosted, isotonically calibrated outage-risk models ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). CPU-only.
- **Data:** ERCOT real-time and day-ahead prices, EAGLE-I county outages (CC BY 4.0), Open-Meteo weather (CC BY 4.0), NWS alerts via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet archive, and NREL ResStock loads ([data card](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet/blob/main/docs/DATA_CARD.md)). Customer telemetry kept per tenant.
- **App and backend:** Python job API, a queue for replay grids, Postgres for runs and results, a web report viewer with PDF export.
- **Infrastructure and unit cost (estimate):** replay is CPU-bound. The release reports 15.3 hours of summed training time for all 21 PPO runs on one workstation ([README](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet)), and evaluation is lighter than training. A full policy review should cost tens of dollars of cloud CPU (estimate, not quoted from a provider). The real cost is onboarding each operator's data.
- **Reused from the paper:** simulator, controllers, hazard model, bootstrap evaluation, checkpoints, replay bundles and stress suite ([GitHub](https://github.com/MaruthiV/homefleet)).

## Risks and kill criteria

- **Simulated households.** Results rest on simulated loads and county-level outages ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)). Operators may dismiss them until run on their own telemetry.
- **Small, sophisticated buyer pool.** A few hundred megawatts of aggregated capacity in the ERCOT pilot ([ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/30/ERCOT-ADER-Update-Senate-Business-and-Commerce-September-30-2026.pdf)) means few customers, and the largest likely build in-house.
- **Noisy signal.** The headline coverage gain's interval includes zero and reverses in some years and cities ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34779)); "it depends" is hard to sell.
- **Market rule changes.** ADER Phase 4 and ERCOT market redesigns change how fleets earn money ([ERCOT ADER](https://www.ercot.com/mktrules/pilots/ader/)).
- **Liability.** A report that blesses a backup policy could carry legal risk if customers lose power.

**Kill criteria.**

1. **By week 4:** fewer than 2 of 5 contacted fleet operators or ADER participants agree to a demo using their real reserve rule.
2. **By week 8:** no operator agrees to share anonymised telemetry and outage logs for an own-data pilot.
3. **By week 12:** on a partner's own data, the replay's predicted backup coverage differs from their observed outcomes by more than 10 percentage points, or no partner commits to a paid review.

| **Total** | **5.5 / 10** | |

**Business vs Feature vs Demo**

**Business?** Not yet. A company would need many operators paying for repeated reviews; the pool is small, the largest build in-house, and credibility depends on data the customers control.

**Feature?** Yes. "Replay this reserve policy against past storms and prices" is exactly the kind of tab that makes a DERMS, VPP or fleet-management platform stronger. The platform already holds the telemetry and dispatch, and MIT makes embedding easy.

**Demo?** More than a demo. The release reproduces its own tables on a laptop across several cities, years and storms, so a useful tool can exist in two weeks.

**Verdict: Feature · 5.5/10**
